Our Home

Not a hotel, not an Airbnb. A house in South Goa with a verandah, a ceiling fan that clicks, and a kitchen that never really closes.

The house

What it's actually like

Tiled floors, high ceilings, a porch with two plastic chairs that are somehow the most comfortable seats in the world. Dogs wander in. Someone is always making tea. The wifi works until it doesn't.

It's a five-minute walk from the beach and a two-minute walk from somebody who will feed you. That's the whole geography.

What we'll eat out street food

The spice rule. Goan heat is coconut-based and builds slowly — it doesn't hit you at first bite. Start smaller than you think. “Less spicy” is understood everywhere, and nobody will think less of you. Also: the pickle on the side is not a side.

Small print

Five house rules

01

Shoes off at the door. Every door. Nobody will tell you twice.

02

Eat more than you want. One extra spoonful of rice is a compliment to the cook, and the cook is watching.

03

Nap at 3. The house goes quiet. This is not laziness, it's infrastructure. Join in.

04

Tell somebody where you're going. Not for permission — because if you're late, someone's waiting up.

05

Don't help with the dishes. You'll be shooed away, politely, three times. Say thank you instead.

Your room

The basics

  • Fan and AC. The fan makes a noise, and you'll miss it later.
  • Hot water in the morning — tell me if it isn't.
  • Mosquito net or plug-in, whichever you prefer.
  • Your own bathroom. Don't put paper in the bin, use the one provided.
  • Wardrobe space. There's more than there looks.
