06 · The coastline

Beaches

South Goa's coastline is one long beach with eleven different personalities. Here's which is which, and where it's actually safe to get in.

Read the water before you read the beach. Most South Goa beaches have no lifeguards, and the calm-looking ones can have a rip current at the ends. Swim where other people are swimming, never at dusk, never alone, and if the water pulls you sideways, swim parallel to the shore — not back at it.

In practice

Which beach, when

7 – 9am

Palolem, before the boats

The bay is glass. Fishermen are pulling in. You get the postcard without the crowd, and a coffee after.

1 – 4pm

Benaulim, under an umbrella

Our local. Shade, a shack, a book, the sound of a football game behind you. This is the susegad shift.

5 – 6.30pm

Agonda or Cabo de Rama

Wide empty sand or a cliff over the sea. Both are the kind of sunset you'll photograph badly and remember perfectly.

Beach kit

What lives in my bag

  • Reef-safe sunscreen, SPF 50 — reapply, the sun is not joking
  • A cotton sarong: towel, shade, cover-up, picnic blanket
  • ₹500 in small notes for shacks and carts
  • Water — two bottles, not one
  • A dry bag for the phone
  • Mosquito repellent for after sunset

Beach etiquette

Small things that matter

  • Ask before photographing fishermen or kids.
  • No litter, obviously — the cows eat it and then it's a whole thing.
  • Topless sunbathing is technically illegal and culturally not done outside a few resort stretches. Keep it simple.
  • Shacks are seasonal — many close in monsoon, none mind you staying for three hours.
  • Watch your things, not because it's dangerous, but because it's a beach.
